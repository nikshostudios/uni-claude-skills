Martin's Odyssey

A full-stack launch kit for studying, writing, and building with Claude Code — 38 skills, a life vault, and a seven-day flight plan. Built for you by Nikhil. Nothing here needs to happen at once: one small step a day, and by next week this is second nature.

launch — two commands, ever
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nikshostudios/uni-claude-skills/main/install.sh | bash
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nikshostudios/uni-claude-skills/main/install-vault.sh | bash

First command = all 38 skills (Day 1). Second = your vault (Day 3 — no rush). Then open Claude Code and say: Read the HANDOFF.md from the uni-claude-skills repo and be my guide. Your agent will interview you about your semester and take it from there.

the flight plan

One small step a day. That's the whole trick.

You don't need to learn 38 skills. You need one habit per day for a week — each one pays off the same day you learn it. Everything else is reference for later.

day 1 — ignition

Install, and win once.

Run the first install command. Open Claude Code anywhere and have one real conversation about the thing due soonest. That's it — no vault yet, no systems.

say this"Read the HANDOFF.md from the uni-claude-skills repo and be my guide. Here's what my semester looks like…"
day 2 — first orbit

Study something properly.

Pick the topic confusing you most right now. Let Claude find the best video, pull its transcript, and teach it to you with a quiz at the end. One evening, one topic, actually learned.

say this"Find me the best video on ER-diagrams, get the transcript, then teach it to me." (that's /yt-search → /yt-transcript → /teach)
day 3 — the ship

Install your vault. Get interviewed.

Run the second install command, open the folder in Obsidian, then let Claude interview you for ten minutes — your courses, your week, your deadlines. It builds the whole vault around your answers and tells you which skills fit your life.

say this"onboard me into my vault" — inside the vault folder, after install-vault.sh
day 4 — daily rhythm

Learn the two session habits.

End today's work session with /wrap — Claude files what happened into your vault so tomorrow starts warm. And if a long session ever gets sluggish: /checkpoint hands it to a fresh session without losing anything. These two habits are what make everything compound.

say this"wrap" at the end of a session · "checkpoint this" when a session drags
day 5 — cargo

Write something that sounds like you.

Next essay or discussion post: have Claude grill your argument first, build from your own notes, then strip anything that sounds machine-made with /unslop. Your thinking, structured — not generated filler.

say this"Grill me on my essay argument, then run the writing pipeline from my notes."
day 6 — heavy lift

Run a project like a professional.

Take any coding assignment or group project: spec it, split it into tickets, build methodically, then review the architecture before submitting. This is the pipeline that makes group work stop being chaos.

say this"to-spec what we discussed, break it into tickets, then implement ticket one."
day 7 — deep space

Pick one power-up. Ignore the rest.

Codex second opinions, a real browser for your agent, persistent memory — pick whichever solves a problem you actually hit this week. The hangar below explains each. Everything else can wait a month.

say this"Which power-up fits what I struggled with this week?"
the pipelines

Three chains to memorize. (Or just ask.)

study a topic
/yt-search→/yt-transcript→/teach

Find the best video, pull its transcript and digest, get taught until it sticks — then /wrap files what you learned into your vault.

write an essay
/grill-me→/writing-fragments→/writing-beats→/writing-shape→/unslop

Stress-test the argument, mine your notes, build the arc, draft with you steering, then strip anything that sounds machine-made.

ship a group project
/grill-me→/to-spec→/to-tickets→/implement→/improve-codebase-architecture

Idea → spec → tickets your teammates can split → disciplined build → architecture review before submission.

week 2+ — power-ups

The bigger rig, one piece at a time.

Separate installs that upgrade Claude Code itself. Pick one when you hit the problem it solves — never all at once.

setup, then say this

"Get a second opinion from codex on this bug."

Setup: npm i -g @openai/codex, codex login, then a codex companion plugin via /plugin. Two frontier models agreeing on a diagnosis beats one; disagreeing is where it gets interesting.

once installed, say this

"Use ego-browser to open my course portal and pull this week's readings."

Desktop app with isolated "task spaces" that inherit your logins. Ask Nikhil for the app; its skill teaches your agent the rest.

why bother

"What did we decide about the schema last week?" — and it knows.

Marketplace plugin via /plugin. Install it the first time you wish Claude remembered something.

setup, then say this

"Use the tdd skill to build this feature test-first."

Several pack skills come from Matt Pocock's collection; the plugin adds the rest via /plugin from github.com/mattpocock/skills.

someday — the hangar

Advanced ships. Park them for now.

Five repos worth starring today and exploring in a month or three, once the basics are muscle memory. Each one is a serious upgrade in one dimension.

chaseai-yt/claudex-loop

Four-phase plan hardening: two AI models adversarially review and then build each other's plans — whoever built it never grades it. For when a project really matters.

 tt-a1i/archify

Beautiful, verifiable architecture / workflow / sequence diagrams as self-contained HTML with motion. Perfect for system-design coursework and project reports.

 herdrdev/herdr

A runtime for coordinating multiple coding agents. When one Claude isn't enough — big group projects, parallel workstreams.

 firecrawl/anydoc

Convert Word, PowerPoint, Excel, PDF, EPUB → clean Markdown. Feed any course material into Claude or your vault in seconds.

 deepseek-ai/deepseek-harness

"Everything is a plugin" — a modular harness for building your own agent setup. The deep end of the pool; visit when you're building your own rig.
the ground rule

Tools for learning, not shortcuts past it.

This kit helps you understand material, structure your thinking, polish your production, and run projects like a professional. It will not help you evade AI detectors, fabricate citations, or submit work you can't explain — that's built into the skills themselves. If your university asks for AI-use disclosure, disclose. The goal is graduating smarter, with a rig you'll still use at your first job.
